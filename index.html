About the Series

Where coursework becomes scholarship

Institutional Student SeriesC.A.S.E. - the Class Assignment Series · Edition - is a bi-monthly student-edition scholarly series published by the Centre for Fintech and Strategic Business Research.

Every semester, exceptional undergraduate assignments are written, graded, and forgotten. C.A.S.E. exists so that the best of them are not. The series captures end-of-semester work across fintech, strategy, analytics, and applied business research - and gives it a permanent, citable home.

Every accepted piece is reviewed by a faculty-led editorial board, paired with an ORCID iD, registered with a permanent Crossref DOI under prefix 10.67226, and hosted open access on PubPub under a CC BY-NC 4.0 license. Students leave each issue with a citable artifact for a CV, a graduate application, or a personal site.

Scope of the Series

What we publish

C.A.S.E. publishes high-quality undergraduate coursework that is evidence-driven, clearly structured, and useful beyond the classroom - across the full CFSBR remit.

40% Fintech and Digital Finance · 30% Strategy and Market Reports · 30% Analytics and Sustainable Finance

  1. 01
    FinTech and Digital Financial Services

    Digital payments, MFS adoption and consumer perception surveys, agent banking dynamics, financial inclusion case studies, and modern digital banking infrastructure in Bangladesh.

  2. 02
    Strategy and Market Reports

    Industry competitive structure (Porter’s 5 Forces / PESTLE), corporate strategic reviews, brand positioning audits, market entry models, and sector-specific business briefs.

  3. 03
    Business Analytics and Decision Intelligence

    Survey-based empirical assignments, consumer data interpretation, econometric regression outputs, and data-backed managerial recommendations (Excel/SPSS/R/Python).

  4. 04
    Sustainable Finance and ESG

    Climate finance, development economics, impact measurement, and responsible financial systems.

  5. 05
    Applied Research and Policy Notes

    Structured assignment outputs combining business logic, methods, and recommendations for real stakeholders.

Cross-Disciplinary Inclusivity

Bridging Business, Economics, and Computational Engineering.

While anchored in business and finance, C.A.S.E. actively invites submissions from non-business disciplines - particularly Computer Science (CSE), Software Engineering, Data Science, Economics, and Applied Statistics.

We specifically solicit undergraduate technical and systems-level coursework where computation directly intersects with applied financial systems, enterprise strategy, fraud detection algorithms, algorithmic market intelligence, or consumer data models.

Note: Purely theoretical technical work without an economic, financial, or business application lies outside the current editorial remit.

Submission Guidelines

Editorial benchmarks for undergraduate coursework.

01

Author Eligibility

Undergraduate students (BBA, BSc, BA, or equivalent) from any recognized local or international university.

Single author or co-authored group assignments (Maximum 4 student co-authors).

02

Manuscript Length and Format

Length: 1,500 to 4,000 words (including tables and references; excluding abstract).

Abstract: 150 to 250 words with 4 to 6 relevant keywords.

Style Guide: APA 7th Edition citation and referencing format.

03

Coursework Origin

Work must have been originally developed, presented, or graded as part of an undergraduate course curriculum, capstone, or term assignment.

04

The "No-ID" Anonymization Rule

In compliance with global privacy standards, student roll numbers, university registration IDs, and personal phone numbers must be removed from the draft.

Author identity is verified strictly through Name, University Affiliation, and ORCID iD.

Publication Ethics and Governance

A transparent, author-first scholarly framework aligned with global COPE standards.

All four pillars below apply to every submission. The full policy text is mirrored on the PubPub archive for version control.

01

Zero-Tolerance Plagiarism and AI Disclosure

Maximum 15% similarity index on Turnitin (excluding references). Generative AI usage (ChatGPT, Claude, etc.) must be transparently disclosed; AI cannot be listed as an author, and core critical insights must be human-authored.

02

Mandatory Supervisor Authorization

Submissions must carry explicit consent from the course instructor or faculty advisor who supervised or graded the assignment.

03

Corporate Confidentiality

Use of non-public company data or internal corporate records requires explicit institutional authorization or complete anonymization/proxy masking.

04

Author Copyright (The Empowerment Model)

Authors retain 100% full copyright under a Creative Commons CC BY-NC 4.0 license. Authors grant CFSBR a non-exclusive license to archive and mint a permanent Crossref DOI.

How it works

The C.A.S.E. workflow

Editorial clarity, centralised ingestion. The series owns its branding and hosting; CFSBR runs the metadata pipeline and Crossref deposit.

  1. 01
    Step One
    Internal Screening

    The editorial team reviews the draft; a faculty advisor signs the approval letter.

  2. 02
    Step Two
    Frontend Deployment

    The issue landing page goes live on the C.A.S.E. front-end before archive release.

  3. 03
    Step Three
    Metadata Collection

    Title, authors, ORCID, abstract, and references are packaged for ingestion.

  4. 04
    Step Four
    Central Audit

    CFSBR validates metadata, runs the deposit build, and queues for Crossref.

  5. 05
    Step Five
    DOI Activation

    A permanent DOI is registered and resolves to the PubPub archive record.
